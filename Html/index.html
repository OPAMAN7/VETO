How a check runs

  1. Describe the item

    Name, category and price. The category sets a default lifespan, so a phone is not judged on the same clock as a t-shirt.

  2. Commit to a frequency

    Say how often you will really use it and for how long. Cost per use updates as you move the sliders.

  3. Check for a duplicate

    If you already own something that does the job, or it can be borrowed or rented, that lands on the bill.

  4. Read the verdict

    Buy it, wait 48 hours, or skip it - with the numbers behind the call shown next to it.

What the verdict is built from

Three measures, each computed in the browser from what you type. Every one of them is shown to you, so a verdict is never a black box.

Cost per use

Price divided by the number of times you expect to use the item over its life. A cheap thing used once is expensive.

price / (uses per month × months)

Impulse score

A 0 to 100 weighting of price against your budget, whether a duplicate already exists, and how easily the need can be met another way. Two thresholds turn the score into a verdict.

Under 75
75 to 89
90 and over

Redundancy

What you already own, what a friend could lend, and what the same job costs to rent instead of buy.
